# Listening test for #1211 against a quiet Gotham West baseline

The user reports little noise in former apartment 300 at 550 W 45th Street. Floor and exposure are unconfirmed. That lived experience is a meaningful comfort benchmark, but it cannot calibrate decibels or isolate why Gotham West was quiet.

## What the complaints can mean inside the bedroom

| Source | Typical experience to listen for | Main uncertainty in #1211 |
|---|---|---|
| Trucks, private carting, deliveries | Intermittent engine rumble, acceleration, reversing signals, metal/bin impacts; a quiet interval followed by a sharp event | Exact curb/loading location, collection schedule, window seals, receiving elevation and screening |
| Horns / traffic | Short peaks above the traffic bed; potentially annoying at night even when average sound is modest | Window assembly and sightline to the roadway; a higher floor is not a guarantee |
| Music / voices | Often much quieter with windows shut; low bass can remain after intelligible voices disappear | Where the source actually is, frequency content, duration, and whether it is outdoors or another unit |
| Construction | Drilling/jackhammering, equipment or occasional impact; interior works can transmit through connected structure | Active phase, authorized hours, connection to this building and construction duration |
| Bedroom HVAC | Airflow whoosh, compressor start/stop, tonal hum, cabinet rattle or vibration | Actual equipment condition; building-wide manufacturer marketing is not a bedroom measurement |
| Neighbors / corridors | Voices versus footsteps, door slams or impact thumps | Adjacent occupancy and floor/wall details; street complaints do not measure partition isolation |

These are source descriptions, not claims that every sound is present in #1211. The 3D view model is not an acoustic model: visual screening does not quantify transmission, diffraction, reflection or structure-borne sound.

## A practical paired listening sequence

1. Start in the bedroom with windows fully shut and latched, HVAC off, no music/white noise. Listen at the intended pillow position and at the window. Note what is audible rather than only recording a single average number.
2. Briefly open a window, then latch it again. A large change helps distinguish exterior transmission from room equipment; it does not prove nighttime events will be inaudible.
3. Run low fan, then higher fan, then actual heating/cooling long enough to hear a compressor cycle. Compare whoosh, low hum, startup clicks and vibration separately. Switch it off again to avoid the fan masking street noise.
4. Listen at the quietest feasible nighttime opportunity, ideally spanning an actual collection/delivery event, plus a weekday morning construction period. A ten-minute quiet showing has little power to exclude intermittent overnight disturbances.
5. Record any obvious source, its start/end, window position, HVAC setting and room location. A phone recording is useful for repeat comparison, but phone processing and low-frequency limitations make it unsuitable for a confident prediction of sleep disturbance or a regulatory measurement.

Ask the leasing team for current collection/loading locations and hours, the exact bedroom window/HVAC specifications, and known nearby work. Asking for an observation or document is more useful than asking whether the building is simply 'quiet.' No request has been sent on the user's behalf.

## What a fix would actually address

A bad seal or latch is a concrete exterior-noise pathway to inspect. A rattling HVAC cabinet or excessive compressor vibration warrants equipment service. Curtains, rugs and wall absorption may reduce room echo, but cannot be assumed to block truck rumble or structural impacts. White noise adds masking sound; it does not reduce the source. Keep the HVAC intake, discharge and service/filter access clear when choosing the bed position.

## Primary sources

- [NYC DEP residential noise-control guidance](https://www.nyc.gov/assets/dep/downloads/pdf/air/noise/residential-noise-control-guidance-sheet.pdf): source/path/receiver distinction, outdoor ingress, room absorption and equipment isolation.
- [NYC DEP interior HVAC guidance](https://www.nyc.gov/assets/dep/downloads/pdf/air/noise/interior-hvac-noise-control-guidance-sheet.pdf): rotating equipment can transmit sound and vibration through a building; absorptive treatments address reverberation.
- [NYC 311 business-equipment complaints](https://portal.311.nyc.gov/article/?kanumber=KA-01935): DEP must observe a condition to issue a summons. Lack of an observed violation does not establish absence of intermittent sound.
- [Ice Air console operating manual](https://www.ice-air.com/wp-content/uploads/ICE6064-WSHP-CHPW-Manual.pdf), pp. 9–10: intake/discharge/filter access and excessive-noise servicing. Exact #1211 model remains unconfirmed.
